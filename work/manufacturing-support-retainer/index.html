Case study · St. Louis-area industrial manufacturer

From ad hoc support to a predictable monthly retainer.

A long-term client — a St. Louis-area industrial manufacturer — runs a custom in-house application that AlignSoft built and still maintains. In October and November 2026, we moved that relationship from informal, as-needed support onto a formal software maintenance retainer, with a ticket process the client's team can actually use.

Challenge

Support that lived in inboxes and side conversations.

For years, support for the application was ad hoc. Requests arrived by email or in conversation, hours were hard to forecast, and there was no shared view of what was open, what was urgent, or how quickly something would be picked up.

That arrangement works until it doesn't — especially when the application is part of how a manufacturing business actually runs. The client needed a steadier way to ask for help, and AlignSoft needed a clearer way to respond, without turning every request into a one-off negotiation.

Approach

Put the work on a retainer.
With written rules.

We agreed to move the relationship onto a monthly software maintenance retainer instead of handling it request by request. The point was not to add process for its own sake. It was to make cost predictable, keep extra work behind written approval, and give both sides a clear record of what was asked and what was done.

The retainer is built around a small set of commitments both sides can point to:

  • Six hours of support included each month
  • Written approval before any work beyond those hours
  • SLA response commitments by priority
  • Code ownership retained by the client
  • A defined transition-assistance clause if the relationship ends

What we built

Formal ticketing,
tied to the retainer.

Alongside the contract, AlignSoft set up a path from a support email to an Azure DevOps work item — so requests have one place to land, and priority and SLA tracking live next to the work itself.

01

A dedicated support mailbox

A shared support@ mailbox in Microsoft 365 gives the client one address for help, instead of scattering requests across personal inboxes and side threads.

02

Email becomes a work item

Power Automate flows turn incoming emails into Azure DevOps work items automatically, so nothing depends on someone copying a message by hand.

03

Priority and SLA fields

DevOps fields and states track priority and SLA commitments, so both sides can see what is open, what is urgent, and where a ticket stands.

04

Tested end to end

The setup was tested from an email into the shared mailbox through to a work item the team can assign, prioritize, and close.

Results

A visible queue.
And a predictable monthly cost.

Measured outcome numbers are not published here yet. What is already true is that the engagement now has a structure it did not have before.

Why it matters.

Manufacturing software needs a home for support.

A custom in-house application is only as reliable as the support around it. A retainer with a real ticket process gives the people who depend on the software a way to ask for help, and the people who maintain it a way to respond without guesswork.

The client stays in a stronger position.

They own the code, they can see the work, extra hours cannot start without written approval, and there is a defined way to transition if they ever need to take the application somewhere else.

Long-lived business software, a clear monthly agreement, and the Microsoft 365 and Azure tools that make the process hold up — handled by the same consultant who already knows the application.
